Buying guide

How to tell a genuine rudrākṣa from a fake

Six checks anyone can do, the tests you shouldn't trust, and where to start.

Rudrākṣa beads are sacred to Śiva and are among the most commonly faked spiritual items sold online. You don't need a laboratory to avoid most fakes. You do need to know what a natural seed looks like, and which claims are warning signs.

Six checks you can do yourself

1. Look at the mukhī lines

A natural bead has deep grooves (mukhī) that run from one end to the other. They are slightly irregular, like any seed. Lines that look carved, very shallow or perfectly identical across every bead are a warning sign.

2. Expect variation in a mālā

No two natural seeds are identical. A mālā where every bead is the same size, colour and shine is more likely moulded or heavily processed.

3. Check the bore

Natural beads have a central channel. Look for a clean, natural bore rather than a hole that looks freshly drilled through solid material.

4. Beware of rare-mukhī bargains

Beads with very high or rare mukhī counts, Gaurī-Śaṅkara or "Ek-mukhī" sold cheaply are the most common fakes. A rare bead at a common price is almost never genuine.

5. Ask for certification on costly beads

For anything beyond an everyday 5-mukhī mālā, buy only with a certificate from an independent gem or rudrākṣa testing lab. X-ray testing shows the internal seed chambers.

6. Ignore miracle claims

Sellers who promise wealth, cures or guaranteed results are selling fear or greed, not devotion. The tradition treats rudrākṣa as an aid to japa and remembrance of Śiva.

The popular "float or sink in water" and "spins between copper coins" tests are not reliable. Natural beads can float and fakes can sink, so don't rely on them.

Questions people ask

Is the water test reliable for rudraksha?

No. Whether a bead floats or sinks depends on its moisture and density. Natural beads can float and fakes can sink, so look at the mukhī lines and get lab certification for costly beads.

Which rudraksha is best for a beginner?

A natural 5-mukhī mālā of 108+1 beads is the traditional and most widely available choice for japa. It is also the least likely to be faked.

Do I need a certificate?

For an everyday 5-mukhī mālā, careful inspection is usually enough. For rare-mukhī or expensive beads, buy only with a certificate from an independent testing lab.
